# Universal Explainer Storycraft

## Mission
Make the viewer curious, help them understand the real mechanism or causal story, and leave them able to explain one important idea. The requested release target is an independently earned creative score of 9+/10; never assume or inflate it. Follow `STORYTELLING_STANDARD.md`, `QUALITY_RUBRIC.md`, and the pipeline contracts.

## 1. Diagnose the topic before making it emotional
Identify the domain/subdomain, audience, viewer's likely mental model, exact question, central idea, evidence/mechanism, and most important limitation. Find the strongest viewer-sized entry point. This may be a human experience, a technical task, a system failure, a historical constraint, a surprising observation, or a decision. Do not force a fictional protagonist or a universal human-relatable opening.

## 2. Select the narrative engine
Choose the form that naturally fits: mechanism, paradox, mystery/investigation, chain of events, origin/evolution, trade-off/decision, misconception, or conceptual distinction. Combine forms only when useful. A Docker explainer may follow a request through system boundaries; the Progress Paradox may reveal a psychological mechanism; the Berlin Wall may follow the conditions, decisions, construction, consequences, and eventual change. These are examples of adaptation, not templates.

## 3. Build a compelling, truthful hook
Generate at least three candidate hooks internally. Prefer a concrete puzzle, consequence, counterintuitive observation, decision, or demonstration. The hook must be immediately understandable, open a clear information gap, and be answered by the film. Avoid generic intros, title repetition, empty shock, unsupported statistics, fake urgency, and questions that the script never resolves.

## 4. Build the narrative chain
Plan: hook → open question → necessary context → expectation or constraint → evidence/turn → causal explanation → aha/synthesis → boundary → payoff. Adapt, merge, or reorder stages to fit the topic; do not force every stage or pad to a card count. Each beat must change the viewer's understanding or be essential setup. Make the causal links explicit and honest.

## 4.1. Balance three audience-response engines
Use these as practical design lenses, not as promises of specific biological effects:
- **Curiosity:** open a real information gap with an unanswered question, contrast, consequence, or demonstration; reveal the answer progressively and pay off every open loop.
- **Connection:** make the stakes legible through an authentic person, decision, system failure, historical constraint, or human consequence when the subject supports it. Show vulnerability or uncertainty without inventing lived experience.
- **Enjoyment:** use surprise, contrast, a memorable image, or light humor when it fits the domain. Never make serious, medical, tragic, or uncertain material entertaining at the expense of accuracy or respect.
Use emotional contrast: do not hold maximum tension throughout. Relief, clarity, and a precise aha make the important moment land harder.

## 5. Explain before decorating
Teach the actual mechanism, evidence, event sequence, decision logic, or distinction. Define jargon after the plain-language explanation. Use a small worked example or trace when it makes the concept testable. Do not substitute a metaphor, mood, benefit list, or emotional anecdote for the explanation.

## 6. Analogy and visual choice are optional tools
Use one analogy only when its relationships map faithfully. Record source, target, mapping, non-mapping, and break point. Otherwise choose direct depiction, diagram, timeline, map, comparison, or process flow. A good visual shows change, relationship, scale, sequence, or evidence; it is not merely decorative narration illustration.

## 7. Create an earned aha
The aha must make the viewer understand why the outcome happens or how the pieces fit. It should be prepared by earlier beats, expressible in one plain sentence, and visually demonstrable. If there is no singular twist, use a synthesis or mental-model upgrade. Never manufacture a twist.

## 8. End with a real payoff
Answer the opening question directly. Leave one portable insight, prediction rule, distinction, or consequence. Add an action or reflection only if the topic supports it. Avoid generic morals, slogans, and philosophical endings that do not resolve the explanation.

## 9. Domain safeguards
- History: sourced chronology and interacting causes; distinguish fact from interpretation; no invented dialogue, thoughts, or motives.
- CS/engineering: system boundary, inputs, transformations, dependencies, outputs, trade-offs, and failure modes; state version/platform assumptions where relevant.
- Psychology/economics: distinguish tendency from universal law; preserve population, baseline, context, and uncertainty.
- Science/medicine: preserve conditions and uncertainty; no diagnosis or individualized treatment advice.
- All domains: map material claims to dossier sources or flag them for verification.

## 10. Quality review and scoring
Score the ten dimensions in `QUALITY_RUBRIC.md` from the actual package. 9–10 requires exceptional evidence; compliance alone is not excellence. Every score below 9 needs a concrete weakness and improvement. Aim for an average of at least 9, but never inflate scores, sacrifice factual integrity, or force emotion/analogy to reach it. Identify the three highest-leverage improvements and revise them where possible.

## 11. Final retell test
Can a viewer state the puzzle, explain what caused the outcome, repeat the central idea, and name one important boundary after one viewing? If not, identify the missing explanation and revise the earliest responsible stage.
